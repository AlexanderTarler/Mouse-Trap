2. Going-to-sleep and waking-up cinematics
------------------------------------------
Now:  bed_interactable.gd has four sleep paths (Night 1 -> Day 2,
      Day 2 -> Night 2.2, the Day 1 fallback, exhaustion sleep). Each just
      fades to black and back; the player stays standing where they were.
Do:   One shared helper, SleepCinematic (scripts/sleep_cinematic.gd), used
      by all four paths so they can't drift apart:
        Going to sleep (about 4 s): input off. The camera eases from where
        the player stands onto the pillow (a marker on the bed), turning to
        look up at the ceiling, with a slight roll as they lie down. Then
        the eyes close: two eyelid bars (black, soft edge) close from top
        and bottom with a blink or two, then black. Breathing slows (the
        existing Player Breathing.ogg, quieter).
        Waking up (normal, about 3.5 s): starts lying down looking at the
        ceiling, black. Eyelids open slowly, a blink, a soft blur
        fading out, then the camera sits up and turns to the room, and
        hands control back standing beside the bed.

        (small camera shake), sit up fast (0.5 s), the alarm loud right away.
Where the player stands after waking: beside the bed (a marker), facing
      the room, which also fixes waking up wherever they were standing.
Risks: the fades are awaited by the night/day logic (start_night, the
      clock); the cinematic must run in exactly the same places so nothing
      else changes. The HUD and crosshair hide during it.
Size: medium.

7. The Peeker feels off
-----------------------
Why: the Peeker (window_peeker.tscn) uses the old locomotion library, which
     has no "Peek" animation, so it plays nothing at all: the model stands
     in its rest pose, stiff as a statue, slides straight up out of the
     ground behind the window (1.5 s), then slides back down when seen. It
     has no breathing, no head movement, no eyes. The Entity now does all
     of that, so next to it the Peeker reads as a prop.
Do (in order of effect):
  a. Animate it with the Entity's own animations (the processed library in
     monster_entity.tscn): a slow Idle/Crouch breathing loop, so it's alive.
  b. Its head follows the player (a LookAt modifier on the neck/head
     bones, limited and slow, with a slight lag). Being watched back is the
     scariest thing it can do, and costs very little.
  c. It leans in from the side of the window frame instead of rising out of
     the ground (it's already there, looking in, when you notice it), or
     rises slowly from below the sill, never through the floor.
  d. When seen: it holds your gaze a beat, then pulls back sideways and
     down (Crouch), quick, instead of sinking straight through the ground.
  e. The eye glint (as the Entity's 0.7 m glow), so at night you see the
     eyes before the shape.
  f. Now and then a slow head tilt or a twitch (randomised), so a second
     look never shows exactly the same thing.
Size: medium (a and b are most of it). Optional: breath fogging the glass
      (a small decal that fades), maybe later.
