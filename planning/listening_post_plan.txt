LISTENING POST PLAN: something to do at night that keeps you alive
==================================================================
STATUS: plan only. Nothing built yet.

THE IDEA
--------
The nights are reactive: wait, and answer the Entity when it shows up.
The listening post makes them about information. Microphones around the
cabin feed the computer; the player reads where it is and goes there with
the beam, a lit room, planks or the lamp.

The loop:
  a faster, quieter Entity -> you can't track it by ear -> the microphones
  become necessary -> it learns them and goes for them -> a dead microphone
  is a blind spot -> fixing it means going out at night (only when it's
  necessary) -> later, flares make that trip survivable.

Decided:
  1. The portable lamp becomes an everyday light from Night 2. The Day 3
     overload (Arvid's logs) is the twist: "you've used it as a light all
     along; it can do more".
  2. The microphone data shows on the computer's own monitor, in the world.
     computer_ui.tscn IS the monitor's screen, always (also when nobody
     sits at it). Plus a speaker in the cabin that crackles when a
     microphone picks something up.
  3. A handheld receiver: later (part G).

Rules that keep it fair:
  - The microphones never lie about real movement. The deception is its
    mimicry (fake blips), never the system.
  - Some cues only reach you in the room itself (its breath at a window, a
    board creaking upstairs, the snuffling at the door). The screen says
    where; your ears and the cabin say how close.


A. THE COMPUTER SCREEN IN THE WORLD  [the foundation]
-----------------------------------------------------
Now: the monitor shows a static CRT texture (PCInteractable caches
crt_screen_mp_2 and swaps in black without power). ComputerUI is a
full-screen CanvasLayer, hidden until the player sits down (the camera
tweens to the sit marker, the UI appears over everything).

Do:
  - ComputerUI goes into a SubViewport (about 1024x768, the 4:3 of a CRT).
    A CanvasLayer inside a SubViewport draws into that viewport, so the
    scene itself barely changes. The monitor's screen surface gets a
    material with that ViewportTexture (plus a little CRT: scanlines, a
    green-grey glow, curvature), replacing the static texture.
  - It is always visible: the login screen, the desktop, an open window,
    whatever was left on it. No power: black, as now (and the SubViewport
    stops updating: UPDATE_DISABLED, so it costs nothing).
  - Sitting down: the camera tweens to the sit marker so the monitor fills
    the view, and input is forwarded into the SubViewport (keys as they
    are; the mouse: a ray from the camera to the screen mesh, its UV ->
    the viewport's pixel). Standing up: input stops, the screen stays.
  - Far away it renders at a lower rate (UPDATE_WHEN_VISIBLE, and every
    other frame beyond ~8 m) to keep the cost down.

Watch out for:
  - ComputerUI hides itself in _ready (visible = false) and uses
    get_viewport() for the maximize size and the taskbar: inside the
    SubViewport those give the screen's size, which is what we want, but
    every use needs checking.
  - Mouse capture and the HUD toggling on open/close stay as they are.
  - Tests reach it by the "computer_ui" group (ui_test, bugs*, lore_test,
    tasks13...): keep the group; a moved node path is fine.
  - Legibility: the Win95 theme's 11-13 px text at 1024x768 must read with
    the camera at the sit marker. Check by screenshot, and at the web
    build's resolution.
  - Web: SubViewport and ViewportTexture work in Compatibility.

Test: the monitor shows ComputerUI with nobody seated (its texture is the
SubViewport's); log in by forwarded input; stand up and the desktop stays
on the screen; generator off -> black; on -> the same desktop back.


B. THE MICROPHONES
------------------
What they are: the Institute's acoustic kit. Officially for the colonies'
nocturnal activity (the memo's version); really, it's what they've wanted
recordings of all along. The player turns their tool against it.

  - A case of 5 microphones (ItemData "Microphone"), by the computer.
  - Placed like the lamp: carried, put down outside, on a short stand, its
    light green while it hears. Within 30 m of the cabin (the cable
    reaches the junction box by the generator; a thin line on the ground).
  - Each hears a circle of HEAR_RADIUS (start: 14 m) around itself.
  - Picks up: the Entity moving (loud), its mimicry (a fake blip at the
    sound's spot), the player moving outside (so you can test it by day),
    deer/crows (rare, small: noise that teaches you to read the blips).
  - Doesn't pick up: the Entity standing still. That is its counter.
  - Introduced as a Day 1 task after the first login ("Set up the
    microphones": the office's MEMO.TXT gets a line about it), so Night 1,
    when it only watches from the treeline, is the tutorial: you watch it
    circle on the screen while it can't reach you.

Where it hooks in: a Microphone node per placed mic (group
"microphones"); each physics tick (or every 0.2 s) it checks the Entity's
distance and speed; EntityAudio.mimic reports its fake sounds to the mics
in range; RoutineManager gets the Day 1 step; Checkpoint saves where they
stand and whether they work.


C. LISTEN.EXE
-------------
A program on the LUND desktop. Opened once, it stays open on the monitor
(part A): you leave it running and glance at it from across the room.

  - A top-down outline of the cabin, the mics as dots, and around each a
    ring that pulses when it hears something: brighter for louder, and a
    trail that fades over ~6 s, so you see direction of travel.
  - Each mic: OK / NO SIGNAL. A dead mic's sector goes grey.
  - Green phosphor on black (its own look, inside the Win95 window).
  - A clock and a small log of the last few blips ("02:14 MIC 3").


D. THE SPEAKER
--------------
A small speaker on the computer desk, wired to the mics: a crackle and a
click when one picks something up, louder for a louder blip. Heard from
anywhere in the cabin (it's the reason to go and look). Caption:
"[the speaker crackles]". The player can turn it down (it can be heard
outside too: noise_radius, a "speaker" kind, small).


E. THE ENTITY: FASTER, QUIETER, AND AGAINST THE MICS
----------------------------------------------------
Only once the mics work (tuned with night3_sim).
  - Faster while unseen: observe/approach/stalk/search speeds up (start:
    x1.3), back to now while the player looks at it or it's in the beam.
  - Quiet far away, clear up close: its footsteps barely heard beyond
    ~10 m, clear within ~4 m (FootstepAudio's unit_size/max_distance, and
    the "far off it is heard" rule in update_footsteps reversed).
  - Learns the mics, by night:
      Night 1: ignores them.
      Night 2: stops still near one now and then (no blip), and its
               mimicry can come from a mic's area.
      Night 3: tears one or two out (a new state, like SABOTAGE on the
               fuse box): a last loud blip, then NO SIGNAL.
    A dead mic is fixed by going out to it with a spare (or re-setting
    the one on the ground): the necessary trip outside.


F. THE LAMP AS AN EVERYDAY LIGHT (the twist)
--------------------------------------------
Most of this exists: the lamp, the cable to the generator outlet (20 m),
the magenta wall switch in the computer room (LightSwitchSpecial), its
fuel drain while on (0.5/s, 4x a cabin light), and the Entity going for a
lit lamp (ATTACK_LAMP; 3 hits pull the cable out).
  - It's available from Night 2 (found in the barn on Day 2, with a cable
    spool), not only on Day 3's side track. The side track's "Find
    portable light" and "Find cable spool" tick off if they're already
    found.
  - On and off only from inside: the wall switch (as now), and nowhere
    else (no switch on the lamp).
  - Day 3, Arvid's logs: the same lamp, overloaded, is the trap. The
    willow plan doesn't change.
  - With the mics: you hear it go for the lamp, then decide: switch it off
    (save fuel, lose the light) or let it take the hits. A pulled cable is
    another necessary trip outside.


G. LATER
--------
  - The handheld receiver: a battery box with 8 lights and a crackling
    speaker (a scanner, a baby monitor). Shows the mics while you're out.
    Runs on the flashlight's batteries; in the hand instead of the
    flashlight.
  - Flares: steady light, so a safe circle it won't enter (the old journal:
    a flash maddens it, a steady flood holds it). 2-3 a night, 30-60 s;
    the glow is seen from far (it knows you're out); when it dies you're in
    the dark and it's angry. Resolve the old unused TACTICAL_LOG_REWARDS
    text in computer_ui.gd (it has the Entity attacking flares): make it
    match, or remove it.


ORDER
-----
  1. A: the computer screen in the world. On its own already better (the
     desktop on the monitor as you walk past), and everything else needs
     it.
  2. C with fake data (a debug button to fire blips): get the look right
     on the monitor.
  3. B + D: real microphones, the Day 1 step, the speaker. With today's
     Entity (still loud): try it, tune HEAR_RADIUS and the count.
  4. E: speed, quiet, the mic sabotage. night3_sim for the numbers, then a
     playtest.
  5. F: the lamp from Night 2.
  6. G when 1-5 have been played.

Each step: its own test (tests/listening_test.gd growing), the full suite,
perf_benchmark before/after (the SubViewport is the new cost), and the
web build checked in a real browser.


OPEN QUESTIONS
--------------
  - 5 mics, or fewer (4) so there's always a blind side?
  - Do they come with the cabin (the case by the computer), or in the van
    (the Institute's kit, unloaded on Day 1)?
  - Should the screen show the player's own blips at night (inside: no;
    outside: yes, so it's clear the system hears you too)?
  - The speaker: always on, or a switch (silence at the price of not
    hearing it)?
