THE ENTITY WHILE THE PLAYER IS INSIDE — why it wears off, and what to do
=======================================================================

       widens it for the charge flare.

The short version: the Entity is scariest as a possibility and least scary
as a visitor. Right now, once the player is inside, it mostly becomes a
visitor: it shows up the same way, makes the same noises in the same order,
walks around in plain view, and plays by rules the player learns in one
night. Every clear look at it spends some of its power, and the player gets
a lot of clear looks.

The fixes below aim at one idea: hear it more, see it less, and never let
the player be sure where it is or what it will do next.


1. WHAT IT DOES NOW (player inside)
-----------------------------------
  OBSERVE   watches from the treeline, crouched; confidence rises.
  APPROACH  walks to a window (shy now: avoids the player's room, slips away
            when spotted, sometimes lures with knocks).
  PEEK      stands at the glass, taps when unwatched, ducks when looked at.
  BREACH    hits the glass, then the planks: loud, slow, telegraphed.
  ENTER     climbs in.
  HUNT      walks a fixed tour: remembered hiding spots, then every room,
            stopping 3.5 s in each to look around. Charges if it sees the
            player within 9 m in the dark. A player in a lit room is safe:
            it stands in the dark and stares.
  FLEE      leaves after 45 s without contact, usually back out of the
            window it came in through.
  Plus: floor creaks from the other floor, murmurs, presence flicker of lit
  rooms (outside only), light sabotage from outside (Night 3), traces.


2. WHY IT GETS LESS SCARY
-------------------------
2.1  It's seen too much, too clearly
       - It carries its own light: ProfileGlow, a green OmniLight with a 6 m
         range on its head. In a dark cabin that announces it from across
         the room and lights its whole body.
       - In HUNT it walks room to room in plain view, at walking pace,
         stopping to "look around" in doorways. Each sighting shows the model,
         the loop of its walk, how it turns: the things that make it a game
         character rather than a thing in the dark.
       - Waiting outside a lit room, it stands there staring, visible.
2.2  It's predictable
       - The break-in is always the same song: taps, then hits on the
         glass, then plank after plank, then the climb. The player hears it
         coming every time and has seconds to prepare.
       - HUNT is a tour; FLEE is "after 45 s, leave". Once the player has
         seen one cycle, they know the whole night.
2.3  The rules are too safe
       - A lit room is 100% safe. The scariest place in a horror game should
         never be completely safe; here it is a bunker.
       - The overcharged flashlight always drives it off indoors.
2.4  Its sounds tell the truth
       - Footsteps play whenever it moves, at any distance, so the player
         can always track it by ear. It never goes quiet, and it never lies.
       - When it leaves, it really has left.


3. WHAT TO DO (by impact)
-------------------------

3.1  Kill the glow, keep the eyes  [small, big effect]
     Remove ProfileGlow's body light (or shrink it to a faint 0.6 m eye
     glint visible only when the player is close or shining a light). In the
     dark it should be a shape, found by the flashlight, not a green lamp.
     Keep the charge flare (set_eye_glow) as the one moment it lights up.

3.2  Indoors, it hides too  [medium]
     The shyness it now has outside, inside:
       - Seen at a distance (not charging): it steps back out of the doorway,
         behind a wall or the stairs, within a second. The player saw
         "something". They'll doubt it was even there.
       - Peripheral, not direct: it's allowed to be in the corner of the
         view (in_player_view) but avoids the centre (player_watching). The
         glimpse is scarier than the stare.
       - Waiting outside a lit room: out of the line of sight, beside the
         door frame, not standing in the open. The player knows it's there
         (they heard it), but can't see it.

3.3  Ambush instead of patrol  [medium, very effective]
     Replace much of HUNT's tour with waiting:
       - It picks a dark spot on the player's likely route (by the door of
         the room it last heard them in, the top of the stairs, a corner the
         flashlight has to sweep), crouches, and doesn't move or make a sound
         for a long time (20-60 s).
       - The player walks into it, or the flashlight finds it at 3 m. That's
         a scare that works the fifth time too, because the player never
         knows which corner.
       - The new Crouch animation is exactly right for it.

3.4  Silence means close  [small]
     Its footsteps should fade out as it nears the player while unseen, and
     stop entirely within ~6 m when it's creeping. Far away, it's heard
     (good: tension). Close, nothing. The player learns that silence is the
     worst sound in the house.

3.5  It lies  [medium]
       - Fake departure: sometimes when it "gives up", the player hears it
         leave (the window, footsteps outside, a growl in the yard) while it
         stays inside, in an ambush spot (3.3). The relief is the trap.
       - Mimicry: now and then it makes the player's own sounds elsewhere in
         the house: their footsteps upstairs, the notebook being flipped, a
         light switch clicking, a knock on the front door in the player's
         rhythm. Use rarely; it's a "did I just hear that?" scare.
       - Phantom presence before it's inside: a creak or a footstep inside
         the cabin while it's actually still at the treeline.

3.6  More ways in, fewer warnings  [medium]
       - A quiet entry: an unboarded window it opens (or an unlocked door it
         just walks through) without breaking anything. The player can't
         count on hearing the breach.
       - Varied breach rhythm: a hit, a long silence, then three fast hits;
         sometimes it stops halfway and the player doesn't know whether it
         left.
       - Windows of rooms the player isn't in (already, via shyness), so the
         player often discovers it's inside rather than hearing it arrive.

3.7  Safe rooms, slightly less safe  [small to medium, late game only]
     Night 3 only, so the rule holds long enough to be learned first:
       - It can reach in and switch off a light from inside the next room
         (the switch click is heard), or trip the room light with the
         presence flicker from indoors, not only from outside.
       - It taps on the wall of the lit room from the dark side, or breathes
         right behind it.
       - The light still protects, but the player has to keep it on (fuel)
         and can't stop listening.

3.8  It leaves marks the player finds  [small]
     Traces exist (prints, gouges). Add a few indoor ones that say "it was
     right here while you were somewhere else":
       - doors (the front door) left open that the player closed;
       - an object knocked over (a physics prop nudged);
       - prints that stop at a wall, or lead into a room and not out.

3.9  Escalate across the nights  [small]
     Each night should add one new behaviour rather than all at once:
       Night 2: only peeks and taps (already); first glimpse.
       Night 3: entry, ambush, silence-when-close, fake departure, light
                tricks. Mimicry once or twice at most.
     Fewer, bigger moments per night, with quiet between them (the
     confidence rhythm already supports this).


4. SUGGESTED ORDER
------------------
  Round A (the biggest return, low risk):
    3.1 no glow, 3.4 silence when close, 3.2 hiding indoors.
  Round B (the new scares):
    3.3 ambush, 3.5 fake departure, 3.6 quiet entry and broken rhythm.
  Round C (the finishing touches):
    3.7 safe rooms less safe (Night 3), 3.5 mimicry, 3.8 marks, 3.9 pacing.

  Choices that are yours:
    - Should lit rooms stay fully safe on Night 2, and only get "less safe"
      on Night 3? (I'd say yes.)
    - Mimicry is the most unsettling idea here, and the easiest to overdo.
      Once or twice a night at most?
    - The fake departure can feel unfair if it then kills the player
      instantly. Should an ambush give a tell (a breath, a creak) a second
      before it lunges? (I'd say yes: a fair scare is a better scare.)
