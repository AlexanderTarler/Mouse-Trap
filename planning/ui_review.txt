UI REVIEW — what to improve, and why
===================================
Scope: everything the player reads, clicks or watches: the HUD, the
crosshair and interaction prompts, the inventory, the stash (scrap heaps),
notes and the notebook, tutorial hints and thoughts, the menus and settings,
the opening, the death sequence and the endings.

The goal: the game should be easy to read and hard to get stuck in, while
the screen stays quiet and the dread stays high. Every point below is
either a bug, a "the player is confused here" problem, or a place where the
UI breaks the mood. They are ordered by impact within each section.
STATUS: all three rounds are done (tests/ui_test.gd). Left for later:
sound captions (from 2.7).


1. BUGS AND BROKEN BITS (fix first; mostly small)
-------------------------------------------------

1.1  The UI doesn't scale with the window  [biggest one]
     project.godot sets no stretch mode, so the UI is drawn in raw pixels for
     a 1920x1080 screen. Now that Windowed / Borderless / Fullscreen exist,
     anyone not on exactly 1920x1080 gets:
       - tiny text and bars on a 1440p or 4K screen;
       - menus cut off or crowding in a small window;
       - the inventory off centre: its panel sits at fixed pixel offsets
         (541, 278), not anchored to the middle.
     Fix: display/window/stretch/mode = "canvas_items", aspect = "expand"
     (the UI is laid out once for 1080p and scaled to any screen), and anchor
     the inventory panel to the centre like the stash now is.

1.2  Raw markup in prompts
     The interaction prompt is a plain Label, so four prompts show their
     formatting tags literally while the player is exhausted:
       "[color=gray]I am too tired to do this[/color]"
     (scrap heap, wrecks, notes, the totem). Use a RichTextLabel for the
     prompt, or drop the tags.

1.3  The stash shows an internal name
     Its title reads "Scrap Heap — scrap_heap_01". The player should see a
     place, e.g. "Scrap Heap — Barn", "Scrap Heap — Wreck Yard", or simply
     "Scrap Heap".

1.4  The inventory panel is half empty and half broken
       - An empty bordered box (InfoBox) sits under the grid: its labels are
         never filled. The item's name and description show at the top
         instead, in 12 px text.
       - 10 slots in an 8-column grid: a row of 8 and a row of 2, inside a
         grid area tall enough for five rows.
       - "Use" is enabled for every item, but only batteries and notes do
         anything. Pressing it on a plank, fuel can or key silently does
         nothing.
       - The close button is a 28 px "x".
     Fix: 5 x 2 slots, the name and description in the info box, Use greyed
     out (or relabelled: "Read", "Swap battery") per item.

1.5  Prompts offer [E] when E does nothing
     "[E] Generator (Full)", "[E] Fuse Box (Active)", "[E] Window (Secured)",
     "[E] Need Cable Spool", "[E] Can't board this window yet": these are
     statuses, not actions. Show them without the key, dimmed. Holding is
     worded four different ways: "[Hold E] Unlock Door", "[E] Hold to Refill
     Generator", "[E] Install X (1/3) [Hold 1.5s]", "[E] Boarding... 40%".
     One rule: "[E] Do thing" for a tap, "[Hold E] Do thing" for a hold.

1.6  Note pages close on any left click
     A stray click (players click to "use" things) throws the note away. Close
     on Esc / E at the last page only, or put a close button.

1.7  Placeholder ending text
     "MEDIOCRE ENDING — The player escaped the cabin, but the Entity
     remained... The player disappeared after a few days and were never seen
     again." It speaks of "the player", in the third person, and calls the
     ending mediocre. With "GOOD ENDING" and "Ending 1 of 2", it breaks the
     spell at the very moment the game should land (see 3.3).

1.8  Names that don't match
       - The death screen says "Night 2" (the raw night counter) while the
         HUD and notebook say "Day 2".
       - NightLabel's fallback reads "Night 1 / 5": the game has three days.

1.9  Emoji in the HUD
     "🔋 100%", "⚡ 100%", "⛽ 100%", "⚠️ BATTERY LOW" rely on the system's
     emoji font. They look out of place next to everything else, and in the
     web build they can show as empty boxes.


2. EASIER TO PLAY (fewer moments of "what now?" or "how?")
----------------------------------------------------------

2.1  Swap the battery with one key
     Today, a dying flashlight means: Tab, find the battery, click it, click
     Use, Tab, while it is dark and something is coming. A key (R: "change
     batteries") that swaps in a spare at once, with the swap sound, removes
     the most fiddly moment in the game. Keep the inventory route too.

2.2  Notice when the objective changes
     The objective line at the top changes silently; players miss it. When it
     changes: a pencil-scratch sound, a brief brighten or "underline" sweep,
     and "Notebook updated" for a moment. Small, and in keeping with the
     notebook.

2.3  One hold indicator
     Holding E (boarding, refuelling, installing parts, unlocking, purging the
     totem) shows progress as a percentage inside the prompt text; the totem
     has its own bar. Use one thin ring that fills around the crosshair for
     all of them. It's the convention players know, and it keeps the eyes
     centred.

2.4  A crosshair that tells you something
     It's a large white shooter cross, always on, and it looks the same
     whether you're aiming at a fuse box or a tree. A small dot that grows
     slightly and brightens over something you can use (and dims when the
     prompt says you can't) reads instantly and suits the genre better.

2.5  Item pictures instead of abbreviations
     No item has an icon, so slots show "BATT", "PLANK", "FUEL". Thumbnails
     can be rendered automatically from each item's 3D model (they all have
     one: scene_to_spawn), once, at startup. No art needed.

2.6  A controls page in the pause menu
     J (notebook), Hold F (overcharge), Ctrl (crouch), Q/E (pages), Tab, R...
     are each shown once by a tutorial hint and then never again. A
     "Controls" panel in the pause menu, plus a footer in the notebook and
     inventory, lets players look things up instead of guessing.

2.7  Settings players expect (and a dark game needs)
       - Brightness / gamma: the single most important one for a game this
         dark; without it some screens show nothing and others give it all
         away. A "make the symbol barely visible" calibration screen on first
         launch is the genre standard.
       - Mouse sensitivity, and invert Y.
       - Head bob on/off (motion sickness), and field of view.
       - Optional sound captions for the cues that matter ("[knocking at a
         window]", "[car alarm]", "[footsteps nearby]"): accessibility, and a
         safety net for players with the sound low.

2.8  A tip on the death screen
     After a death, one line about what happened or how to survive it ("It
     can't see you crouched in a bush." / "Light keeps it out of a room." /
     "Boarded windows slow it down."). It turns a frustrating death into
     learning, without a tutorial.

2.9  Say that cutscenes can be skipped
     The death scene can be skipped with any key, but nothing says so. The
     opening's "[Space] Skip" is at 35% opacity in a corner. On a replay, the
     endings should be skippable too.


3. TRUER TO THE GENRE (quieter, more in-world)
-----------------------------------------------

3.1  A quieter HUD
     Green/yellow/red progress bars are survival-shooter language. In this
     game:
       - Stamina: no bar until below 50% (it already hides when full); then
         a thin dim line, with the breathing (already there) and a slight
         vignette doing the talking.
       - Battery: a small flashlight glyph with 3-4 segments, shown while the
         light is on; it flickers at the last segment (the beam already
         flickers).
       - "Day 2": shown as a title card at dawn, then gone. It doesn't need
         to sit in the corner all day.
     What stays: the objective line, prompts, thoughts, hints.

3.2  One visual language
     Right now each screen has its own look: the default Godot font on the
     HUD and menus, Georgia italic for thoughts, a paper notebook, a
     Windows 95 computer, a brown "scrap" stash and a dark inventory. The
     notebook and the PC are great as they are (they're objects in the
     world). For the rest, bundle one font with character (a typewriter or
     field-journal face such as Special Elite or Courier Prime, both free) for
     the HUD, prompts, inventory and menus. Make the inventory a canvas field
     bag and the stash its counterpart. A bundled font also removes the
     web/emoji risk (1.9).

3.3  Endings that land
     The cutscenes ("It Follows", "Dawn") are strong; the screen after them
     is a placeholder. Suggestion:
       - Title them by name: "It Follows" / "Dawn", not MEDIOCRE / GOOD.
       - Epilogue on the phone from the opening (it bookends the game):
           It Follows: days later, the Institute texts "Lund? You haven't
             reported in." ... "Lund?" - unread. The phone screen cracks to
             black.
           Dawn: signal returns on the road; a text from Arvid's number,
             delivered late, or the player's "Arrived" finally sending.
       - Then "Ending 1 of 2 — there is another way out." in small type, so
         players know to look for it.

3.4  A death screen in the world
     "YOU DIED / The darkness claims another soul" is generic. The death
     cutscene already ends under the willow. A crossed-out notebook line
     ("Field survey suspended — researcher missing") with "Restart from
     this morning" / "this evening" (whichever the checkpoint is) fits better
     and tells the player exactly what restarting costs.

3.5  The opening, readable
     The phone's text is 14 px (11 px for the status line) on a 1080p screen;
     it's the one place the story is spelled out. 18-20 px, and a slightly
     longer hold on the office message, which is the important one. Consider
     "press any key to continue" on the phone instead of fixed timings, so
     slow readers aren't rushed.

3.6  Pickup messages
     "Added Fuel Can to inventory" in a bright line at the bottom is the one
     place the game sounds like a menu. A short, dim line ("Fuel can") with a
     soft pick-up sound does the same job.


4. SUGGESTED ORDER
------------------
  Round 1, small and safe (an afternoon):
    1.1 scaling, 1.2 markup, 1.3 stash title, 1.4 inventory layout,
    1.5 prompt wording, 1.6 note clicks, 1.8 names, 2.2 objective notice,
    2.9 skip hints.
  Round 2, play feel:
    2.1 battery key, 2.3 hold ring, 2.4 crosshair, 2.6 controls page,
    2.7 brightness and the other settings, 2.8 death tips.
  Round 3, mood:
    3.1 quieter HUD, 3.2 font and styling, 3.3 endings with the phone
    epilogue, 3.4 death screen, 3.5 opening, 3.6 pickups, 2.5 item icons.

  Choices that are yours (I'd recommend the first of each):
    - The inventory doesn't pause the game. That's good tension; keep it,
      but the battery key (2.1) makes it fair.
    - Captions (2.7): on by default only for the "something is at the
      window" cues, or off entirely by default?
    - Endings: the phone epilogue, or a short text card in the Win95 style
      of the computer?
